## Tank room and survival assays

**Source:** Jesse Lowe, 2026-08-21 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/08/21/tank-room-and-survival-assays-2/
**Finding:** In a 22-hour mortality check of heat-challenged Agate Pass cockles, only two individuals remained alive, and one of them — from the heat + polyIC primed group — was still moving and responding to stimuli, suggesting combined thermal and immune priming may extend survival under acute heat stress.

### Supports [bioRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed]: Parental immune priming reshapes offspring growth, metabolism, and thermal tolerance in the Pacific Oyster
Baird et al., 2025 · DOI: 10.64898/2025.12.10.693539 · https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.10.693539

Broodstock Pacific oysters were given a Poly(I:C) immune challenge and their offspring were reared and then tested for survival, growth, and metabolic response under elevated temperature. Offspring of primed parents showed 35% lower mortality than controls at 40 °C, along with altered metabolic activity and differential expression of heat shock (HSP70, HSP90) and immune (viperin) genes — but the benefit reversed at 42 °C, where primed offspring died at a 17% higher rate. This is the closest published analogue to the lab's heat + polyIC group surviving longest, and it also flags that any priming benefit is bounded by temperature, which matters for interpreting a single survivor at one heat dose.

### Supports [PubMed]: Immune priming and haemolymph-mediated protection in Mytilus edulis against Vibrio splendidus
Ben Cheikh et al., 2026 · PMID: 42288298 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42288298/

Blue mussels pre-exposed to live or heat-killed Vibrio splendidus survived a later bacterial challenge at markedly higher rates, with protection persisting at least 21 days. Transfer of cell-free plasma or haemocytes from primed animals conferred protection to naïve mussels, and primed animals showed sustained haemocyte increases and altered phagocytic and lysosomal activity. It establishes that a priming treatment in a bivalve can translate into a measurable survival difference — the outcome the cockle mortality check is set up to detect, albeit here against a pathogen rather than heat.

### Supports [PubMed]: Immune priming reshapes the microbiota and modulates pathogen dynamics in the Manila clam (Ruditapes philippinarum)
Rodino-Janeiro et al., 2026 · PMID: 42039805 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42039805/ (originally posted as bioRxiv preprint: https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.27.696656)

Manila clams primed orally with a sublethal dose of Vibrio europaeus and then given a lethal secondary challenge showed 87% survival versus 0% in unprimed clams, and cleared the pathogen below the mortality threshold within 48 h. Priming also restructured the host microbiota toward a distinct, persistent bacterial community absent in unprimed animals. The magnitude of the survival split shows how large a priming effect can be in a clam when group sizes are adequate, underscoring that the lab's single responsive survivor is suggestive rather than quantitative.

### Adds context [PubMed]: The specifically enhanced immune response of oyster Crassostrea gigas against the secondary encountered same pathogen
Zuo et al., 2026 · PMID: 41407080 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41407080/

Pacific oysters primed with either Vibrio splendidus or Staphylococcus aureus and then re-challenged with V. splendidus mounted enhanced phagocytic, antimicrobial, and gene-expression responses that were specific to the priming agent, alongside broad increases in histone modifications after the first stimulation. Priming in bivalves is therefore not a generic "toughening" — the identity of the primer shapes what protection is gained. That bears directly on whether a polyIC (viral-mimic) primer should be expected to help against a purely thermal challenge, or only in combination with heat conditioning.

### Adds context [PubMed]: The RNA sensor MDA5 contributes to the antiviral immune response in Crassostrea gigas by modulating the MAVS-mediated signaling pathway
Xu et al., 2026 · PMID: 41605267 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41605267/

This work identified an MDA5 homologue in Pacific oyster whose expression in haemocytes rose after poly(I:C) and dsRNA stimulation, and showed it binds long dsRNA and interacts with CgMAVS to drive CgIRF and IFN-like gene expression. It maps the receptor-level route by which a polyIC injection is actually detected in a bivalve. Knowing that polyIC engages a defined RNA-sensing cascade helps frame what a heat + polyIC primed animal has been physiologically committed to before the heat challenge begins.

### Adds context [PubMed]: An OASL homologue involved in IFN-like antiviral signal by binding MDA5 in the Pacific oyster Crassostrea gigas
Zeng et al., 2026 · PMID: 41580101 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41580101/

An OASL homologue in Pacific oyster was found to be upregulated in haemocytes after poly(I:C) and recombinant IFN-like protein stimulation, to bind dsRNA, and to interact specifically with CgMDA5 rather than CgRIG-I. This places OASL downstream of the same polyIC-triggered IFN-like axis and identifies concrete transcripts that respond to the primer. Those transcripts are candidate readouts if the cockle assay is later paired with expression work to explain why primed animals persist longer.

### Suggests next step [PubMed]: CgSMARCC2 modulates chromatin accessibility to regulate haemocyte proliferation in the immune priming of oyster Crassostrea gigas
Zhou et al., 2026 · PMID: 42162879 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42162879/

The authors characterized a SWI/SNF chromatin-remodeling subunit in Pacific oyster that is induced days after a first Vibrio stimulation and, when knocked down, reduces chromatin accessibility at immune gene promoters and blunts haemocyte proliferation on secondary challenge. It offers a mechanism by which a priming exposure leaves a durable, days-long molecular trace rather than a transient one. If the heat + polyIC survival difference holds up with more animals, this points to chromatin accessibility and haemocyte proliferation as the next things to measure in the primed cockles.

**Literature summary:** Three peer-reviewed studies establish that priming a bivalve can produce a real, sometimes very large survival advantage on a later challenge (87% vs 0% in Manila clams; sustained protection in blue mussels), and one preprint tests the specific combination at issue here — Poly(I:C) priming and subsequent heat stress in Pacific oysters — finding 35% lower mortality at 40 °C but a reversal at 42 °C. The mechanistic peer-reviewed work shows polyIC is sensed in oysters through a defined MDA5/OASL IFN-like axis and that priming effects are agent-specific and epigenetically encoded, so a viral-mimic primer should not be assumed to confer generic thermal protection. Taken together, the literature makes the lab's observation plausible while making clear it is not yet evidence: a single responsive survivor out of two cannot distinguish a priming effect from chance, and the preprint's temperature reversal suggests the result may depend heavily on the exact heat dose used.
