### The Pathogen Has a Plan, the Hosts Do Not
- **Author**: Steven Roberts
- **Date**: 2026-09-11
- **Categories**: Genomics
- **Key finding**: Comparing the newly confirmed sea star wasting disease agent (*Vibrio pectenicida* FHCF-3) against the genomes of three sea star species spanning a susceptibility gradient (sunflower > ochre > leather star), the pathogen's virulence arsenal is rich and complete — including 16 pore-forming toxin/hemolysin loci and two intact aerolysin-family toxins with fully functional secretion systems (T2SS, T3SS, two T6SS clusters). On the host side, none of 50 tested gene families survive multiple-testing correction, so the susceptibility gradient is not explained by gene content; GPI-anchor and glycosphingolipid/sphingomyelin biosynthesis pathways (candidate toxin-receptor routes) are intact and undifferentiated across all three species, ruling out simple receptor-loss models. The strongest surviving candidates (not statistically significant but ranked by effect size) are a host aerolysin-like β-PFT gene family and C-type lectins, both following the resistance gradient, motivating planned RNA-seq and coelomocyte toxin-sensitivity follow-up experiments.
