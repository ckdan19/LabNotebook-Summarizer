### Adds context [Research Square preprint — not peer-reviewed]: The Effects of High Carbonate Alkalinity under Low Salinity on the Survival, Physiological Metabolism, and Redox Homeostasis of Crassostrea hongkongensis
Nan et al., 2026 · DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10038928/v1 · https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10038928/v1

This preprint exposed the Hong Kong oyster (*Crassostrea hongkongensis*) to elevated carbonate alkalinity (10 or 20 mmol/L) under low salinity and measured survival, oxygen consumption, ammonia excretion, and hemolymph ammonia alongside redox/energy-metabolism markers. Both alkalinity treatments suppressed early oxygen consumption and ammonia excretion and altered feeding allocation, with the higher dose significantly reducing survival and driving stage-specific redox/energy-metabolism stress responses. This supports treating disrupted water chemistry — elevated alkalinity alongside elevated ammonia/nitrite/nitrate — as a genuine physiological stressor capable of disrupting normal ammonia handling in bivalves, lending plausibility to the trial tank's abnormal readings as a real stress condition rather than a benign fluctuation.
