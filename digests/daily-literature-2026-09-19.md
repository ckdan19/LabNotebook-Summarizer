Sun et al., 2026 · PMID: 42650228 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42650228/

Using transcriptomics and RNAi knockdown, this study identified CgPDIA6 as a hub regulator that protects Pacific oyster haemocytes from heat-induced oxidative damage by sustaining antioxidant enzyme (SOD1, SOD2, PRDX6) activity, with silencing worsening oxidative injury under 30°C heat exposure. This supports the general principle behind the notebook's data — that Pacific oysters mount specific, tunable transcriptional programs in response to heat stress — though it examines a different regulatory node than the priming-related dampening seen for HSP70/HSP90/ATP synthase/citrate synthase.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Microbial endolithic symbiosis in oysters enhances thermal resistance through shell corrosion
Zardi et al., 2026 · PMID: 42430847 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42430847/

This study found that Pacific oysters (Magallana gigas) colonized by shell-corroding endolithic symbionts had significantly higher survival under heat stress, with corrosion-related shell lightening providing a thermal buffer as high as 9.5°C in the field. This is a structural rather than transcriptional heat-tolerance mechanism, but it shows Pacific oysters have multiple, non-exclusive routes to enhanced thermal resistance, suggesting the notebook's priming-related dampening of stress-gene expression may be one of several mechanisms this species draws on under repeated heat exposure.

### Adds context [bioRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed]: Parental immune priming reshapes offspring growth, metabolism, and thermal tolerance in the Pacific Oyster
Baird et al., 2025 · DOI: 10.64898/2025.12.10.693539 · https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.10.693539

This preprint found that Pacific oyster offspring from immune-primed parents had 35% lower mortality than controls at 40°C but 17% higher mortality at 42°C, with altered HSP70, HSP90, and viperin expression under heat stress relative to controls. Although the priming stimulus differs (parental immune challenge vs. direct heat pre-exposure), it shows that priming effects on stress-gene expression and thermal tolerance in this species are real but temperature-limited, consistent with the notebook's own observation that priming dampens — rather than eliminates — the heat-stress transcriptional response.
