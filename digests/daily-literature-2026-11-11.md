## Guava Muse: system check and preliminary testing

**Source:** Christina Zhang, 2026-11-11 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/11/11/guava-muse-system-check-and-preliminary-testing/
**Finding:** Using the Guava Muse MitoPotential kit on room-temperature (unstressed) oysters, few cells showed mitochondrial depolarization and most cells remained viable; paired heat-stressed oysters (36°C for 2 hours) could not be assayed this session due to a technical issue mixing stock solutions with cells.

Adds context [PubMed]: Tissue-Specific Trade-Offs Between Biomineralisation and Antioxidant Responses in Magallana gigas Infected with Boring Sponges Pione vastifica
Kladchenko et al., 2026 · PMID: 42193218 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42193218/

This study measured intracellular ROS and mitochondrial membrane potential in hemocytes of Pacific oysters (Magallana gigas) infected with boring sponges, alongside heat-shock protein and antioxidant-enzyme responses in mantle tissue. Infection significantly raised hemocyte ROS levels but left mitochondrial membrane potential unchanged, even as heat-shock protein expression dropped across tissues. This shows that hemocyte mitochondrial membrane potential can remain stable under one physiological stressor (sponge-driven oxidative stress) while other stress markers shift, supporting the plausibility that the room-temperature oysters' stable MitoPotential readings reflect a genuinely low-stress baseline rather than an assay insensitivity, though the stressor tested here (infection) differs from acute heat exposure.
