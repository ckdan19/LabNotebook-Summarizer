## qPCR Analysis — M.gigas PolyIC Data from Valentina's Project

**Source:** Sam White, 2026-02-02 · https://robertslab.github.io/sams-notebook/posts/2026/2026-02-02-qPCR-Analysis---M.gigas-PolyIC-Data-from-Valentinas-Project/
**Finding:** In *Magallana gigas* qPCR of Valentina's PolyIC project, HSP70 is driven by temperature stress (≈32-fold up under heat, but suppressed by PolyIC alone) while the antiviral gene VIPERIN is upregulated by PolyIC (≈2.3-fold, p=0.01) and cGAS is essentially unresponsive to PolyIC — indicating pathway specificity, with the PolyIC-driven antiviral (VIPERIN) response decoupled from the cGAS DNA-sensor and from the thermal HSP70 response.

### Supports [PubMed]: An OASL homologue involved in IFN-like antiviral signal by binding MDA5 in the Pacific oyster Crassostrea gigas
Zeng et al., 2026 · PMID: 41580101 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41580101/

This study identified CgOASL, an interferon-stimulated gene (ISG) in the Pacific oyster (*Crassostrea gigas*), and showed its transcription in haemocytes rose significantly after poly(I:C) stimulation, downstream of the oyster IFN-like protein and its receptor. Because VIPERIN is likewise an interferon-stimulated antiviral effector, the induction of a sibling ISG by poly(I:C) in the same species reinforces that a dsRNA mimic drives the interferon/ISG arm of oyster immunity. That directly parallels the lab's observation of PolyIC-driven VIPERIN upregulation.

### Supports [PubMed]: The RNA sensor MDA5 contributes to the antiviral immune response in Crassostrea gigas by modulating the MAVS-mediated signaling pathway
Xu et al., 2026 · PMID: 41605267 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41605267/

The authors characterized CgMDA5, a cytoplasmic RIG-I-like dsRNA sensor in Pacific oyster, and found its expression and downstream MAVS–IRF–IFN-like signaling were activated by poly(I:C). This identifies the RNA-sensing route by which a dsRNA mimic such as poly(I:C) triggers antiviral/interferon output in this species. It supports the lab's pathway-specificity reading: PolyIC engages the RLR RNA-sensing axis that would drive ISGs like VIPERIN — a route distinct from the cGAS DNA-sensing pathway, which stayed flat.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Functional analysis of IRF1/2 homolog in immune regulation of the sea urchin Strongylocentrotus intermedius
Liu et al., 2026 · PMID: 42372330 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42372330/

In the sea urchin *Strongylocentrotus intermedius*, a newly cloned IRF1/2 transcription factor was upregulated by poly(I:C) and LPS and could drive interferon-pathway promoters (ISRE, IFNα/β, NF-κB). IRFs are the transcriptional activators of interferon-stimulated genes, so this describes machinery upstream of effectors like VIPERIN. It adds mechanistic context — in a different marine invertebrate — for how a poly(I:C) stimulus is converted into interferon-pathway gene expression; the species differs from the oyster in the lab finding.

### Adds context [PubMed]: The significant regulatory role of cytochrome P450 in the innate immune response of the Zhikong scallop (Chlamys farreri)
Zhao et al., 2026 · PMID: 42331123 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42331123/

This work in the Zhikong scallop (*Chlamys farreri*) found a CYP450 gene that is induced by poly(I:C) and that, when overexpressed, dose-dependently suppresses ISRE reporter activity driven by poly(I:C) or scallop IRF1. It illustrates that poly(I:C)-triggered interferon-pathway signaling in molluscs is subject to negative regulation. That helps explain why individual poly(I:C)-responsive genes can show very different magnitudes of induction — relevant to interpreting the gene-specific responses in the lab data; the species differs from *Magallana gigas*.

**Literature summary:** Two peer-reviewed studies in the same species (Pacific oyster / *Crassostrea gigas*) show that poly(I:C) activates the RIG-I-like (MDA5–MAVS–IRF–IFN) RNA-sensing pathway and induces interferon-stimulated genes such as OASL, directly paralleling the lab's PolyIC-driven VIPERIN upregulation and consistent with an RNA-sensing route distinct from the cGAS DNA sensor that remained unresponsive. Two additional peer-reviewed papers — in a sea urchin and a scallop — add mechanistic context on the IRF/ISRE interferon machinery that turns poly(I:C) into antiviral gene expression and on how that machinery can be negatively modulated.
