## Tumbling Oysters (Steven Roberts)

### Ploidy and pH Don't Move Methylation, Genotype Does
- **Date**: 10-02-2026
- **Author**: Steven Roberts
- **Categories**: Epigenetics, Computing
- **Key finding**: Reanalysis of the Haws triploid × ocean-acidification WGBS dataset (24 oysters, diploid/triploid × high/low pH) using permutation nulls, power simulations, and variance partitioning found no detectable ploidy or pH effect on methylation at any scale (CpGs, windows, gene bodies, or GO sets) — the earlier reported 178 ploidy / 154 pH differentially methylated loci fall within the shuffled-label null and are most likely false positives. The one robust signal is that methylation similarity tracks genetic background (genotype explains 8-12% of methylation distance, Mantel r = 0.73-0.77), and four genetically distinct oysters happen to all fall in the high-pH group, partially confounding pH with genotype. Next steps are to check hatchery records for the outliers, flag/retract the earlier DML results and downstream annotation, and write this up as a solid null paired with a genotype-methylation finding.

### PolyIC immune priming and thermal tolerance in Pacific oysters

**Source:** Ariana Huffmyer Lab Notebook (10K Seed project final survival assay results)
**Finding:** Baywater 10K seed oysters previously field-outplanted with PolyIC showed the highest thermal/freshwater-stress tolerance (LT50 = 39.2h) versus untreated Control (LT50 = 31h), a significant treatment-group effect on survival (Chisq=12.78, p=0.012).

### [Adds context] [PubMed]: The RNA sensor MDA5 contributes to the antiviral immune response in Crassostrea gigas by modulating the MAVS-mediated signaling pathway
Xu et al., 2026 · PMID: 41605267 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41605267/

Identified and functionally characterized a MDA5-like dsRNA sensor (CgMDA5) in Crassostrea gigas that is strongly upregulated by poly(I:C) stimulation in hemocytes and interacts with MAVS to drive an IRF-IFNLP antiviral signaling cascade; knockdown of either MDA5 or MAVS blunted this response. This maps out the specific molecular mechanism by which poly(I:C) engages the oyster innate immune system, providing a plausible basis for how field PolyIC priming could leave a lasting systemic immune signature — though the paper does not test survival or thermal tolerance directly.

---

### [Adds context] [PubMed]: An OASL homologue involved in IFN-like antiviral signal by binding MDA5 in the Pacific oyster Crassostrea gigas
Zeng et al., 2026 · PMID: 41580101 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41580101/

Characterized CgOASL, an interferon-stimulated gene downstream of the oyster's interferon-like pathway, showing its expression is induced by both poly(I:C) and the oyster's own interferon-like protein, and that it binds dsRNA and interacts with CgMDA5 to support antiviral signaling. Combined with the MDA5 paper above, this further details the durability and breadth of the interferon-like cascade that poly(I:C) triggers in C. gigas — relevant background for interpreting why PolyIC-primed oysters might show altered stress tolerance weeks after exposure, though this study likewise does not test thermal tolerance.

**Literature summary:** Two peer-reviewed papers (Xu et al. 2026; Zeng et al. 2026), both in Fish & Shellfish Immunology, map out the MDA5-MAVS-IRF-IFNLP interferon-like signaling cascade that poly(I:C) activates in Crassostrea gigas hemocytes. Neither paper tests thermal tolerance or survival directly, but both support the premise that poly(I:C) triggers a robust, mechanistically well-defined systemic immune response in Pacific oysters — context that helps explain how PolyIC field-priming could plausibly carry over to affect later heat-stress survival, as observed in Ariana Huffmyer's 10K seed Baywater assay results this week.

### Genotype vs. treatment effects on DNA methylation in oysters

**Source:** Tumbling Oysters (Ploidy and pH Don't Move Methylation, Genotype Does)
**Finding:** Reanalysis of the Haws triploid × ocean-acidification WGBS dataset found no detectable ploidy or pH effect on methylation at any scale; genotype explained 8-12% of methylation distance (Mantel r = 0.73-0.77) and confounded with the pH treatment groups.

### [Adds context] [PubMed]: Prior exposure to hypoxia alters DNA methylation patterns in the eastern oyster
McDonough et al., 2026 · PMID: 41942905 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41942905/ (originally posted as bioRxiv preprint: https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.15.694390)

In a fully factorial experiment on juvenile eastern oysters (Crassostrea virginica) exposed to hypoxia or control conditions at two time points 60 days apart, both the first and second exposure independently contributed to variation in gene-body methylation, and prior hypoxia exposure left a detectable methylation "memory" distinguishable from other treatment histories. This supports the general principle that environmental exposure history shapes oyster methylation patterns — useful context for the Haws reanalysis finding that genotype, not ploidy or pH treatment, is the dominant driver of methylation variance there; together they suggest oyster methylation studies need to carefully separate genetic background from treatment-history effects before attributing differential methylation to an environmental factor.

**Literature summary:** One peer-reviewed paper (McDonough et al. 2026, BMC Genomics; originally posted as a bioRxiv preprint) found that prior hypoxia exposure in eastern oysters leaves a lasting, timing-dependent signature in gene-body DNA methylation. This adds context to the Tumbling Oysters finding that genotype — not ploidy or pH treatment — dominates methylation variance in the Haws dataset: both point to the need for careful separation of genetic background from treatment-history effects in oyster methylation studies.
