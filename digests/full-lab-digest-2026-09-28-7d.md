- **Author**: Sam White
- **Date**: 2026-09-21
- **URL**: https://robertslab.github.io/sams-notebook/posts/2026/2026-09-21-Resazurin-Assay---M.gigas-El-Nino-Conditioning-and-Heat-Stress-at-36C/
- **Categories**: resazurin, Magallana gigas, Crassostrea gigas, Pacific oyster, heat stress, El Niño, SENO
- **Key finding**: Steven wanted to know whether prior "El Niño" conditioning altered the metabolic response of adult *Magallana gigas* (Pacific oyster) to acute 36°C heat stress. ### El Niño conditioning and resazurin heat-stress metabolic response in Pacific oysters

**Source:** Sam's Notebook (Sam White); Genefish WordPress
**Finding:** In adult *Magallana gigas*, prior "El Niño" conditioning did not alter the metabolic response to acute 36°C heat stress as measured by resazurin fluorescence — area-under-curve metabolic rate and the time×treatment interaction were statistically indistinguishable between conditioned (n=20) and control (n=20) oysters.

### Conflicts [PubMed]: Heat priming protects oyster gills from subsequent lethal heat stress: Histopathology and apoptosis evidence in Crassostrea angulata
Chen et al., 2026 · PMID: 41830855 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41830855/

This study exposed *Crassostrea angulata* (a close congener of *M. gigas*) to sublethal heat priming followed by lethal heat stress, then assessed gill histopathology (shrinkage, filament breakage, ciliary loss) and TUNEL-based apoptosis. Priming significantly reduced structural gill damage and cell-death signal compared to unprimed lethal exposure, indicating that prior sublethal heat history measurably changed the tissue-level stress response. This contrasts with the lab's finding of no detectable effect of prior conditioning on resazurin-based whole-animal metabolic rate, though the two studies use different species, different "priming" regimes (sublethal heat vs. an ambient "El Niño" exposure), and different endpoints (histology/apoptosis vs. metabolic fluorescence) — so the discrepancy may reflect assay sensitivity or biological level (tissue vs. whole-organism) rather than a true contradiction.

**Literature summary:** One peer-reviewed paper in a closely related oyster species reports that prior sublethal heat exposure measurably protects gill tissue from subsequent lethal heat stress, based on histopathology and apoptosis markers — a different and more sensitive endpoint than the whole-animal resazurin metabolic-rate measure that found no effect of conditioning in the lab's own *M. gigas* data. No preprint search was possible this week (Europe PMC was unavailable), so this comparison rests on a single peer-reviewed source and should be treated as suggestive rather than conclusive; verifying whether resazurin AUC is simply an insensitive readout for priming effects that show up histologically would help reconcile the two results.
