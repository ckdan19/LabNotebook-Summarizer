### Ploidy and pH Don't Move Methylation, Genotype Does
- **Date**: 10-02-2026
- **Author**: Steven Roberts
- **Categories**: Epigenetics, Computing
- **Key finding**: Reanalysis of the Haws triploid × ocean-acidification WGBS dataset (24 oysters, diploid/triploid × high/low pH) using permutation nulls, power simulations, and variance partitioning found no detectable ploidy or pH effect on methylation at any scale (CpGs, windows, gene bodies, or GO sets) — the earlier reported 178 ploidy / 154 pH differentially methylated loci fall within the shuffled-label null and are most likely false positives. The one robust signal is that methylation similarity tracks genetic background (genotype explains 8-12% of methylation distance, Mantel r = 0.73-0.77), and four genetically distinct oysters happen to all fall in the high-pH group, partially confounding pH with genotype. Next steps are to check hatchery records for the outliers, flag/retract the earlier DML results and downstream annotation, and write this up as a solid null paired with a genotype-methylation finding.
