## Tank room and survival assays

**Source:** Jesse Lowe, 2026-10-05 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/10/05/tank-room-and-survival-assays-12/
**Finding:** During the 24-hour mortality check of an ongoing oyster survival assay, 3 mortalities were recorded across replicate tanks maintained at slightly varying water chemistry (pH 7.6-8.0, nitrate 0-20 ppm, nitrite 0-0.5 ppm).

### Adds context [PubMed]: Bacterial Communities Across the Production Chain of a Pacific Oyster (Magallana gigas) Hatchery During a Larval Mortality Event
Zhang et al., 2026 · PMID: 42654767 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42654767/

This study tracked bacterial communities across an entire hatchery production cycle for Magallana gigas, sampling larvae from three replicate tanks through a mortality event that caused roughly 40% cumulative losses. It found that an unidentified Cryomorphaceae-related bacterium, not Vibrio, dominated during the mortality event, and that mortality and microbial composition varied meaningfully even among replicate tanks reared under nominally identical conditions. This supports reading the lab's own tank-to-tank variation in water chemistry and mortality counts as potentially reflecting differences in microbial community composition as well as the measured water-quality parameters.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Physiological and immunological resilience of the Pacific oyster Magallana gigas (Thunberg, 1793) to fluctuating salinity
Andreyeva et al., 2026 · PMID: 42070746 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42070746/

This study exposed Pacific oysters to a wide range of hypo- and hyperosmotic salinity conditions (8-30 ppt) and monitored mortality alongside hemocyte immune parameters and antioxidant enzyme activity. Despite substantial physiological stress responses at salinity extremes, oyster survival remained at 100% under every tested condition, indicating a considerable tolerance buffer before water-chemistry perturbation alone triggers mortality. This offers a useful comparison for the lab's tanks, where mild water-chemistry variation was accompanied by only a small number of mortalities, consistent with oysters tolerating moderate water-parameter fluctuation without major survival impact.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Safety Assessment of the Extract of Phycobiliproteins Derived From Arthrospira platensis: Acute Toxicity Studies in Pacific Oysters
Kukhareva et al., 2026 · PMID: 41738043 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41738043/

This acute toxicity study exposed adult Pacific oysters to a water-borne extract at three concentrations over 24- and 48-hour periods — directly paralleling the notebook's 24-hour mortality-check design — and found no treatment-related change in oyster mortality or hemocyte apoptosis at any concentration, though sub-lethal cellular stress markers (DNA damage, HSP90 upregulation) did rise at the highest dose. This suggests oysters can sustain measurable physiological stress from water-quality exposures over a 24-48 hour window with low or no overt mortality, consistent with the small mortality count seen here despite imperfect water chemistry across tanks.

**Literature summary:** Three peer-reviewed papers (no relevant preprints were found for this topic) place the lab's mortality count in context: controlled salinity and toxicity studies in Pacific oysters show these animals can tolerate substantial water-chemistry and physiological stress over 24-48 hour windows with low or zero mortality, while hatchery-scale data show that tank-to-tank mortality variability during assays is often linked to differences in microbial community composition rather than water chemistry alone. None of these studies tested the specific nitrate/nitrite levels recorded here, so they provide supporting context rather than a direct mechanistic explanation for the 3 mortalities observed.
