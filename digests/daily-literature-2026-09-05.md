## 09.04: qPCR results for HSP70, HSP90, ATP synthase

**Source:** HazelAbrahamsonA, 2026-09-04 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/09/04/qpcr-results-hsp70-hsp90-atp-synthase/
**Finding:** In a repeated temperature-stress experiment on oysters, an initial heat exposure (32°C, 48h) lowered expression of HSP70, HSP90, and ATP synthase (GAPDH-normalized) relative to no-heat controls; a second heat exposure after a 1-week recovery produced less suppression of HSP70 and HSP90 but greater suppression of ATP synthase.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Tissue-Specific Trade-Offs Between Biomineralisation and Antioxidant Responses in Magallana gigas Infected with Boring Sponges Pione vastifica
Kladchenko et al., 2026 · PMID: 42193218 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42193218/

This study found that Magallana gigas (Pacific oyster) infested by boring sponges showed decreased heat shock protein (Hsp70, Hsp90) expression across all examined tissues, alongside a bioenergetic trade-off that reallocated limited ATP away from the ATP-demanding process of biomineralisation toward antioxidant defense. Although the stressor here is sponge infestation rather than heat, it demonstrates the same coordinated suppression pattern linking heat-shock genes and ATP-dependent energy metabolism seen this week, supporting the idea that HSP and ATP synthase expression are mechanistically coupled under stress in this species.

### Supports [PubMed]: Assessing impacts of extreme climate and weather events on endangered pearl oysters Pinctada maxima
He et al., 2026 · PMID: 41478057 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41478057/

This study exposed pearl oyster (Pinctada maxima) juveniles to simulated marine heatwaves combined with ocean acidification and found that heatwave stress significantly depressed expression of Hsp70, Hsp90, and multiple ATP/energy-metabolism genes (ATP1A, ATP1B, ND5, ATPeV1F, ATPeF1A), with the effect strongest under low pH. This directly parallels this week's finding that heat exposure lowers HSP70, HSP90, and ATP synthase expression together, reinforcing that acute heat stress broadly suppresses the heat-shock/energy-metabolism gene axis across bivalve species, though the species and combined-stressor design differ from the notebook's single-stressor repeated-heat design.

### Adds context [bioRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed]: Parental immune priming reshapes offspring growth, metabolism, and thermal tolerance in the Pacific Oyster
Baird et al., 2025 · DOI: 10.64898/2025.12.10.693539 · https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.10.693539

This preprint (co-authored by members of this lab group) found that Pacific oyster offspring differentially expressed HSP70 and HSP90 alongside shifts in metabolic activity under acute thermal stress, with the direction of the metabolic effect reversing between a moderate (36°C) and a higher (40°C) challenge temperature. It adds context by showing that HSP expression and metabolic/energy state are linked in a non-monotonic, temperature-dependent way in this species, a consideration relevant to interpreting why the second heat exposure this week produced a diverging suppression pattern for ATP synthase versus the HSPs.

**Literature summary:** Two peer-reviewed papers — one on a different stressor (sponge infestation) and one on combined marine-heatwave/ocean-acidification stress — both support the idea that heat-shock proteins (HSP70/HSP90) and ATP-related energy-metabolism genes are co-regulated and suppressed together under stress in bivalves, consistent with this week's coordinated HSP70/HSP90/ATP synthase suppression under repeated heat exposure. An additional preprint from this same lab group — not yet peer-reviewed — shows HSP70/HSP90 expression tied to a non-monotonic metabolic response across temperature in Pacific oysters, a pattern that may help explain why the second heat exposure diverged between the HSPs and ATP synthase.
