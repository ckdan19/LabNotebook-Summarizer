**Finding:** In Round 5 of survival assays for the Manchester sampling experiment, 38 of 75 oysters died by the morning check, requiring an additional evening check to reach near-full mortality — consistent with the mortality-endpoint assay design used across the lab's hardening rounds.

### Suggests next step [PubMed]: From blue to pink: resazurin as a high-throughput proxy for metabolic rate in oysters
Huffmyer et al., 2026 · PMID: 42495017 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42495017/

This study validates a resazurin-based, whole-organism metabolic-rate assay in *Crassostrea gigas* and *C. virginica*, showing resazurin fluorescence tracks oxygen consumption closely, and that in acute thermal-stress trials, individuals showing greater metabolic depression were more likely to survive — i.e., metabolic response predicted eventual mortality outcome. This is directly actionable for the lab's Manchester hardening rounds, which currently require tracking mortality out to a near-full-mortality endpoint over repeated daily checks: a resazurin readout could offer a much faster proxy for the same survival signal in future rounds.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Genomic and Pathogenic Characterization of Vibrio alginolyticus CG-1 Isolated from Crassostrea gigas
Kim et al., 2025 · PMID: 41085765 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41085765/

This paper characterizes a *Vibrio alginolyticus* strain isolated from diseased *Crassostrea gigas* and runs a dose-response pathogenicity assay in oyster larvae, finding dose-dependent mortality with an LD50 of ~5.04×10⁶ CFU/mL, comparable in severity to established pathogens like *V. coralliilyticus*. The stressor (bacterial challenge) and life stage (larvae) differ from the lab's thermal-hardening trial, but the paper offers a useful methodological comparison point — a structured exposure protocol scored to a defined mortality endpoint in the same species — for interpreting mortality curves in the lab's own multi-round survival assays.
