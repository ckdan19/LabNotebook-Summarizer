### What Genes Reveal About Sea Star Wasting Disease
- **Author**: Steven Roberts
- **Date**: 07-10-2026
- **Categories**: Transcriptomics, Genomics
- **Key finding**: Day-12 differentially expressed genes from sunflower, ochre, and leather stars were restricted to ortholog-matched gene families and run through topGO over-representation testing to ask whether the three species respond to wasting disease in the same way. Each species had hundreds of enriched functions (448 / 536 / 348) that were mostly species-specific, but eight functions were over-represented in all three: stress granule assembly, two protein kinase activity terms, ubiquitin-ligase adaptor activity, trans-Golgi network, axon cytoplasm, adherens junction organization, and PPAR signaling. The shared core sketches a coherent picture of stress signaling, protein quality control, and disrupted cell-cell adhesion — the most likely candidates for a disease-central response rather than a species quirk.

### Three Ostrea chilensis Assemblies Walk Into a Dot Plot
- **Author**: Steven Roberts
- **Date**: 08-20-2026
- **Categories**: Genomics, Computing
- **Key finding**: A dependency-free Python script ran three minimap2 `-x asm5` whole-genome comparisons (~72 min on 16 threads, 68–97 GB RSS) among the merged "Assembly 1.0" and the two haplotype assemblies to justify a reference choice. Assembly 1.0 turns out to be a mosaic: four sequences are byte-identical to HapA chromosomes, two to HapB, and chromosomes 1 and 7 have both haplotypes concatenated into single sequences (~175 Mb of duplicated reference, bad news for RNA-seq multi-mapping); HapA and HapB also disagree about which chromosome is 3 versus 6, leaving Assembly 1.0 with two chromosomes named 6 and none named 3. HapB is unexpectedly 1.13 Gb versus HapA's 790 Mb, likely uncollapsed repeat, so HapA is the clean haploid option pending a rerun at a looser preset and a check of where existing multi-mapping falls.

### 62 CpGs Separate Quihua From Rio Pudeto
- **Author**: Steven Roberts
- **Date**: 08-21-2026
- **Categories**: Epigenetics, Genomics
- **Key finding**: Whole-genome bisulfite data from two Chilean *Ostrea chilensis* sites (n = 4 each, aligned to Och_HapB) yielded 62 differentially methylated CpGs at q < 0.01 and |Δ| ≥ 25 out of 492,229 tested, split evenly by direction, with a median effect of 62 percentage points and near-binary separation between sites; global methylation was unchanged, so the signal is locus-specific. Destranding CpG dyads before filtering added 26% more testable sites, and a 1 kb tiled DMR analysis (138 DMRs) provides the key validation — 20 of the 62 DMLs fall inside a DMR versus 0.068% of background (OR 697), all direction-concordant. No feature or CpG-island class is enriched after FDR, chromosome 1B is a significant hotspot (28.9 DML per 100k, q = 0.0095), and the 15 DML-carrying genes collapse to 12 non-redundant loci; the author explicitly flags the lone GO hit (aromatase activity) as non-robust and notes that site is confounded with every environmental and genetic variable.
