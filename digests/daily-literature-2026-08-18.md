## Resazurin stability experiment and survival assays

**Source:** naomikang44, 2026-08-17 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/08/17/resazurin-stability-experiment-and-survival-assays/
**Finding:** A resazurin 96-well plate refrigerated since 8/3 was re-read on a Synergy HTX plate reader at the three-week mark as part of a month-long test of how resazurin's validity as a viability/metabolic indicator changes after cold storage. In parallel, thermal survival assays are running on 20 oyster families (n=80) held near 36 °C, with no mortalities yet observed at the first checkpoint.

### Adds context [PubMed]: A Universal Resazurin-Based Viability Assay for Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells in 2D and 3D Cultures
Cervantes-Rivera et al., 2026 · PMID: 42516579 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42516579/ (originally posted as bioRxiv preprint: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.13.718248)

This protocol paper characterizes the resazurin reduction assay as a sensitive, low-background, non-destructive readout in which viable cells reduce blue resazurin to fluorescent resorufin, allowing the same samples to be measured repeatedly over time without lysis. That emphasis on reproducibility and real-time monitoring speaks directly to what "validity" means for a resazurin plate, framing which assay properties a cold-storage stability test like this one should be checking. It does not, however, evaluate signal retention after refrigerated storage, which is the specific gap the lab's month-long experiment addresses.

### Adds context [bioRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed]: From Blue to Pink: Resazurin as a High-Throughput Proxy for Metabolic Rate in Oysters
Huffmyer et al., 2025 · DOI: 10.1101/2025.11.06.686367 · https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.06.686367

This study adapts the resazurin assay to whole oysters (*Crassostrea gigas* and *C. virginica*) and shows resazurin fluorescence correlates with oxygen consumption, validating it as a metabolic-rate measure at the organismal level. It further links metabolism to thermal performance — identifying optima and tipping points — and finds that oysters showing greater metabolic depression are more likely to survive acute thermal stress, with detectable family-level variation across 50 selectively bred families. Both threads map onto this post: it supports resazurin as a valid oyster metabolic/viability indicator (the reagent under stability testing) and provides a directly comparable framework for the 20-family, ~36 °C thermal survival assays running alongside it, though it does not test how the reagent behaves after weeks of refrigeration.

**Literature summary:** One peer-reviewed protocol paper (Bio Protoc) establishes the resazurin reduction assay as a reproducible, non-destructive viability readout, clarifying the assay properties a stability test should verify but not addressing cold-storage signal retention. One bioRxiv preprint (not peer-reviewed) independently validates resazurin fluorescence against oxygen consumption in *Crassostrea* oysters and connects metabolic depression to thermal-stress survival across families — supporting the reagent's validity in this exact organism and offering a parallel to the concurrent 36 °C survival assays. Neither directly tests resazurin stability after three-plus weeks of refrigeration, so the lab's month-long experiment is filling a genuine methodological gap.
