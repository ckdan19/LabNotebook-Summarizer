## Tank room, SHORE room and water chemistry trials

**Source:** Jesse Lowe, 2026-11-20 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/tank-room-shore-room-and-water-chemistry-trials-3/
**Finding:** A dedicated water chemistry trial tank continues to show degraded water quality (pH 7.6, 40 Alk, 0.25+ ppm ammonia, 0.5 ppm nitrite, 200+ ppm nitrate) while standard holding tanks remain near zero ammonia/nitrite, with mortality checks completed across all tanks.

### Adds context [PubMed]: The ammonia transporter gene family and its immune regulatory role in the Pacific oyster (Crassostrea gigas)
Sun P et al., 2026 · PMID: 41861978 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41861978/

Sun et al. characterized the ammonia transporter (AMT) family in C. gigas and showed CgAMT1 is haemocyte-enriched and upregulated under pathogen stimulation and sustained Vibrio exposure, coinciding with progressive ammonia accumulation and haemocyte death. This supports interpreting continued low mortality in the trial tank cautiously: sustained ammonia loading can drive immune-cell-level stress well before it shows up as survival loss.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Moderately elevated salinity rapidly promotes oyster meat yield by rewiring ammonia transport and metabolic networks
He G et al., 2026 · PMID: 42815400 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42815400/

He et al. measured NH4+/H+ ion flux and ammonia-transport gene/enzyme activity in oysters across a salinity gradient, finding transport capacity peaks at moderate salinity and is impaired at the extremes. This offers mechanistic context for the trial tank's ammonia handling: an oyster's ability to actively excrete ammonia is itself condition-dependent, so the trial's degraded water chemistry could be compounding ammonia buildup beyond the raw water-column concentration alone.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Seasonal dynamics of host-associated microbiome and potential human pathogen in Crassostrea ariakensis and Perna viridis
Huang J et al., 2026 · PMID: 41830897 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41830897/

Huang et al. surveyed oyster- and mussel-associated microbiomes across seasons and found ambient ammonia nitrogen and nitrate were among the environmental factors shaping potential-pathogen abundance (including Vibrio spp.) in the host microbiome. This suggests the trial tank's sustained high ammonia/nitrate load could be shifting the resident microbiome toward higher pathogen burden even while overt mortality stays low — a pathway distinct from, and additional to, direct ammonia toxicity.

**Literature summary:** Three peer-reviewed PubMed papers bear on this finding; no relevant bioRxiv/Europe PMC preprints were found. None directly replicate the lab's exact stressor combination, but together they suggest two ways degraded water chemistry could be producing physiological cost not yet visible in mortality counts: ammonia-driven haemocyte stress (Sun et al.) and microbiome/pathogen shifts tied to ambient ammonia and nitrate (Huang et al.), compounded by salinity-dependent limits on the oyster's own ammonia-excretion capacity (He et al.).
