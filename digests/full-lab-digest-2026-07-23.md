### Predictive Resazurin Phenotyping Using VIMS Oyster Family Data
- **Author**: Ariana Huffmyer
- **Date**: 2026-07-23
- **Categories**: resazurin, metabolism, cvirginica
- **Key finding**: Using 4-hour resazurin metabolic curve data from 50 *C. virginica* families (25 high-salinity, 25 low-salinity origin), this analysis found that family identity explains 15–19% of individual variation in metabolic curve shape, with the most informative features differing between phenotype groups — early-phase metabolic rate best discriminates high-salinity families, while sustained late-phase AUC best discriminates low-salinity families. Critically, low-salinity survival performance can be reliably predicted from resazurin curves in low-salinity-origin families (LOFO-CV Spearman rho ~0.53 using a multi-feature composite index), while high-salinity survival prediction fails cross-validation, suggesting the two survival outcomes are governed by fundamentally different physiological mechanisms. These results support resazurin as a practical early-screening tool for low-salinity survival but indicate that a different assay design — potentially longer or thermally challenged — is needed to predict high-salinity performance.

[bioRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed] **Supports**: From Blue to Pink: Resazurin as a High-Throughput Proxy for Metabolic Rate in Oysters
Huffmyer et al., 2025 · DOI: 10.1101/2025.11.06.686367 · https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.06.686367

This preprint by the same research group validates resazurin fluorescence as a direct correlate of oxygen consumption in oysters and demonstrates that the assay detects heritable genetic variation in metabolic performance across multiple experimental designs. It establishes the methodological foundation for the current predictive phenotyping analysis: if resazurin curve shape reflects genuine metabolic differences between families, then the differential predictive power observed across salinity-origin groups is likely a real biological signal rather than assay noise. The current week's analysis meaningfully extends this foundation by identifying which specific curve features — early-phase rate vs. late-phase AUC — carry predictive information in each phenotype group.

**Literature summary:** One preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) from the same research team directly validates the resazurin assay underlying this week's predictive phenotyping work, confirming that fluorescence tracks oxygen consumption and captures heritable metabolic variation in oysters. No peer-reviewed papers indexed on PubMed in the last 12 months specifically addressed resazurin-based metabolic phenotyping for oyster survival prediction, consistent with this being an actively developing method. The combination of the preprint validation and the current family-level predictive analysis strengthens the case for resazurin as a practical early-screening tool, while the failure of high-salinity survival prediction suggests that assay duration or stressor design may need to be tailored to the specific performance trait of interest.
