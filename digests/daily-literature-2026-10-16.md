## Oyster priming day 3 and 4

**Source:** giselff250455090, 2026-10-15 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/10/15/oyster-priming-day-3-and-4/
**Finding:** During day 3 of hydrogen peroxide priming, a refractometer error caused the bath salinity to drop to 20ppt (versus the intended 34ppt) for the duration of that day's 1-hour immersion; no oyster mortality was observed on either day, and salinity was corrected to 33ppt on day 4.

### [Adds context] [PubMed]: Factors Influencing the Clearance of Polystyrene Microplastics by Oysters: Roles of Environmental Conditions, Food Availability and Selection
Li & Pan, 2026 · PMID: 42742753 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42742753/

Tested how temperature, salinity, and food conditions affect microplastic clearance in the oyster *Crassostrea hongkongensis*, finding that hyposaline conditions (5 psu) significantly depressed filtration activity, much like extreme thermal stress did. This suggests oysters can show measurable physiological responses (reduced filtration) to low salinity well short of lethal levels, relevant context for interpreting the lab's brief, accidental salinity dip even though no mortality occurred.

### [Adds context] [PubMed]: Navigating salt gradients: Transcriptomic strategies of bivalves for surviving salinity stress in aquatic systems
Alba et al., 2026 · PMID: 41793930 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41793930/

Synthesized 32 transcriptomic datasets across 18 bivalve species, including *Crassostrea gigas*, and found that hyposalinity consistently induces antioxidant defenses, osmolyte biosynthesis, and chaperone-mediated protein stabilization. Because the lab's priming protocol is already imposing oxidative stress via hydrogen peroxide, this raises the possibility that the accidental salinity drop activated an overlapping antioxidant response that could have interacted with the intended H2O2 stressor, even without causing mortality.

### [Adds context] [PubMed]: Microgeographic variation in gene expression plasticity under osmotic stress in wild eastern oyster juveniles (Crassostrea virginica)
Simon et al., 2025 · PMID: 40669247 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40669247/

Found that low-salinity exposure differentially affects gene expression plasticity in oyster spat depending on population source, and notes that low-salinity conditions are a known contributor to mortality in the species more broadly. This provides background indicating that salinity-stress responses and mortality risk vary substantially with duration, life stage, and population origin — context worth keeping in mind if a similar refractometer error recurs.

**Literature summary:** Three peer-reviewed papers (Li & Pan 2026; Alba et al. 2026; Simon et al. 2025) establish that hyposalinity is a biologically active stressor in oysters — triggering antioxidant and osmolyte responses and reducing filtration — even when it falls short of causing mortality. None directly tests a combined hydrogen-peroxide-plus-brief-hyposalinity exposure, but together they suggest this priming trial's accidental salinity dip was not physiologically inert and may have interacted with the intended oxidative-stress treatment.
