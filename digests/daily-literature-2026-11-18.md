### [Adds context] [Research Square preprint — not peer-reviewed]: The Effects of High Carbonate Alkalinity under Low Salinity on the Survival, Physiological Metabolism, and Redox Homeostasis of Crassostrea hongkongensis
Nan X et al., 2026 · DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10038928/v1 · https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10038928/v1

Nan et al. exposed the related Hong Kong oyster (*Crassostrea hongkongensis*) to low salinity combined with elevated carbonate alkalinity (10 or 20 mmol/L) and measured survival, oxygen consumption, ammonia excretion, and redox/energy biomarkers. The higher-alkalinity treatment significantly reduced survival and suppressed oxygen consumption and ammonia excretion, while the lower-alkalinity group retained partial compensatory capacity. This suggests that in a related species, impaired ammonia excretion under elevated-alkalinity, low-salinity hydrochemical stress can precede outright survival loss — relevant context for the lab's trial tank, where degraded water chemistry (elevated ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, high alkalinity) has so far produced few mortalities, though the exact stressor combination and species differ.
