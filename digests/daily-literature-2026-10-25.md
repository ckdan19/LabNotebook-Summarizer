Adds context [Research Square preprint — not peer-reviewed]: The Effects of High Carbonate Alkalinity under Low Salinity on the Survival, Physiological Metabolism, and Redox Homeostasis of Crassostrea hongkongensis
Nan et al., 2026 · DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10038928/v1 · https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10038928/v1

## Heat stress experiment with oysters concluded

**Source:** giselff250455090, 2026-10-24 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/10/24/heat-stress-experiment-with-oysters-concluded/
**Finding:** On October 23, 2026, the three oysters remaining in the incubator — from the "low #1," "control #1," and "high #3" groups — had all died by 96 hours, concluding the heat stress experiment with complete mortality among the final survivors spread across every remaining treatment group.

Adds context [PubMed]: Microbial endolithic symbiosis in oysters enhances thermal resistance through shell corrosion
Zardi et al., 2026 · PMID: 42430847 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42430847/

This lab and field study found that Pacific oyster (Magallana gigas) individuals whose shells were corroded by endolithic microbial symbionts survived heat stress significantly better than uncorroded oysters, with a thermal buffering effect as large as 9.5°C in the field tied to lighter, more reflective shell coloration. For the lab's incubator assay, this suggests that uncontrolled variation in shell condition or symbiont load among individuals — rather than treatment group alone — could help explain why the final three deaths were spread across the low, control, and high groups instead of concentrated in one.

Supports [PubMed]: Integrated metabolomics and flow cytometry assessment reveals immune-metabolic modulation of Crassostrea (=Magallana) gigas hemolymph under hypoxic and elevated temperature conditions
Kim et al., 2026 · PMID: 42134734 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42134734/

Using Pacific oysters exposed to hypoxia alone or combined with elevated temperature (28°C) for 13 days, this study found hemocyte mortality was significantly higher under combined heat-plus-hypoxia than hypoxia alone, with metabolic shifts suggesting energy conservation came at the cost of immune function. This supports the broader pattern that sustained elevated temperature drives mortality in Pacific oysters, consistent with the lab's own prolonged 36°C incubation culminating in the complete loss of its remaining animals.

Supports [PubMed]: Short-term heat stress adaptation in intertidal oysters (Crassostrea sikamea): Integrative biochemical, transcriptomic and metabolomic insights
Liu et al., 2026 · PMID: 41401620 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41401620/

This study found Kumamoto oysters tolerate moderate warming with minimal mortality but suffer sharply increased death once exposure nears their upper thermal limit — survival dropped to 14% after just 1 hour at 45°C, with any higher temperature or longer exposure uniformly lethal. This parallels the lab's own trajectory toward complete mortality of the final survivors after extended incubation near a comparably extreme temperature, supporting the idea that sustained thermal stress near an oyster's upper tolerance limit produces an abrupt endpoint rather than a gradual decline.

Adds context [EcoEvoRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed]: Intertidal Exposure Modulates Time-Integrated Heat Tolerance of the Eastern Oyster Crassostrea virginica
Villeneuve et al., 2026 · DOI: 10.32942/x2cw9d · https://doi.org/10.32942/x2cw9d

Applied to the lab's own extended continuous 36°C exposure, this framework suggests the experiment's final 100% mortality among survivors is consistent with a duration-dependent survival curve finally reaching its endpoint, rather than a sudden, unexplained event.
