## Mortality Checks

**Source:** maddyab, 2026-09-30 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/09/30/mortality-checks-2/
**Finding:** During a multi-day thermal hardening survival assay (round 1, 10K), oyster mortality was low at T4 (1 mort) and T29.5 (0 new morts) but rose sharply to about 24 new mortalities by T48 hours post-exposure.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Integrated metabolomics and flow cytometry assessment reveals immune-metabolic modulation of Crassostrea (=Magallana) gigas hemolymph under hypoxic and elevated temperature conditions
Kim et al., 2026 · PMID: 42134734 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42134734/

This study exposed Pacific oysters to combined hypoxia and elevated seawater temperature and tracked hemocyte and metabolomic changes over 13 days, finding hemocyte mortality was significantly higher under the combined heat/hypoxia stress and linking this to a metabolic-immune trade-off that the authors suggest contributes to summer mortality events. It does not replicate the notebook's specific hardening-assay timeline, but it supports the general pattern that thermal stress drives a measurable rise in oyster cell/organism mortality over a multi-day exposure window, consistent with the jump from near-zero to ~24 deaths seen here.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Short-term heat stress adaptation in intertidal oysters (Crassostrea sikamea): Integrative biochemical, transcriptomic and metabolomic insights
Liu et al., 2026 · PMID: 41401620 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41401620/

This study ran a survival assay on Kumamoto oysters (a close relative of the Pacific oyster) exposed to short bouts of high temperature, finding minimal mortality below 45°C but only 14% survival after 1 hour at 45°C and 0% survival beyond that point — a sharp, threshold-like transition rather than a gradual one. That pattern of near-zero mortality followed by a steep jump once a stress threshold is crossed parallels the notebook's own observation of 0 new morts at T29.5 followed by ~24 new morts by T48, suggesting the lab's assay may be capturing a similar threshold effect in its own hardening protocol.

**Literature summary:** Two peer-reviewed papers (no relevant preprints were found for this topic) support the general pattern in the notebook entry: oyster mortality under thermal stress tends to stay low until a threshold of cumulative heat exposure is crossed, after which it rises sharply. Neither paper reports the exact hardening-assay protocol used here, so they provide supporting context rather than a direct replication.

## Tank room and survival assays

**Source:** Jesse Lowe, 2026-09-29 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/09/29/tank-room-and-survival-assays-9/
**Finding:** Following acute exposure to 36°C, oysters in the hardening/survival assay showed 30 mortalities at the 24-hour mortality check.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Identifying the regulatory network of the key lipid metabolism transcription factor peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor in oysters
Du et al., 2026 · PMID: 42331158 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42331158/

This study identified PPARα as a key transcriptional regulator of lipid metabolism and thermal adaptation in oysters, showing that the relatively cold-tolerant *Crassostrea gigas* has higher PPARα expression and promoter activity than *C. angulata*, linked to reduced expression of an inhibitory transcription factor. This offers a molecular mechanism that could underlie variation in heat tolerance between oyster lines or individuals, relevant background for interpreting why some oysters in the notebook's 36°C exposure survived to 24 hours while others did not.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Integrated metabolomics and flow cytometry assessment reveals immune-metabolic modulation of Crassostrea (=Magallana) gigas hemolymph under hypoxic and elevated temperature conditions
Kim et al., 2026 · PMID: 42134734 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42134734/

As above, this study found significantly higher hemocyte mortality in Pacific oysters under elevated temperature stress, attributing it to a metabolic-immune trade-off. It supports the broader expectation that acute heat exposure, as used in this notebook's 36°C assay, drives measurable oyster mortality.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Short-term heat stress adaptation in intertidal oysters (Crassostrea sikamea): Integrative biochemical, transcriptomic and metabolomic insights
Liu et al., 2026 · PMID: 41401620 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41401620/

As above, this survival assay in a closely related oyster species found a sharp threshold transition in mortality once a critical temperature/duration combination was exceeded (14% survival at 45°C for 1 hour, 0% beyond), rather than a gradual decline. It offers comparative threshold data useful for interpreting how 36°C exposure, a notably lower temperature, produced substantial (30-count) mortality by 24 hours in this notebook's assay.

### Adds context [EcoEvoRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed]: Intertidal Exposure Modulates Time-Integrated Heat Tolerance of the Eastern Oyster Crassostrea virginica

This preprint built thermal death time curves for the eastern oyster under immersed and emersed conditions, finding that relative thermal tolerance between the two exposure types flips above 37°C, and that survival is highly sensitive to small increases in temperature near that range. The 36°C exposure temperature used in the notebook's assay sits just below this reported tolerance-inversion point, making this a directly relevant (though not yet peer-reviewed) framework for interpreting why substantial mortality (30 morts) emerged by 24 hours at that temperature.

One preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) adds directly relevant context by modeling a tolerance inversion near 37°C in a related oyster species, bracketing the exposure temperature used here.
