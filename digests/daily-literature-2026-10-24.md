### Adds context [PubMed]: The ammonia transporter gene family and its immune regulatory role in the Pacific oyster (Crassostrea gigas)
Sun et al., 2026 · PMID: 41861978 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41861978/

This study characterized the ammonia transporter (AMT) gene family in Pacific oysters and found that sustained ammonia exposure (via long-term pathogen stimulation) drove progressive ammonia accumulation, haemocyte death, and strong upregulation of the ammonia transporter CgAMT1, implicating an ammonia-associated immune cell death mechanism. This provides a mechanistic basis for why the lab's water-chemistry trial tank — with detectable ammonia (0.25+ ppm) alongside zero alkalinity — could be imposing physiological and immune stress on the oysters housed there, beyond a simple water-quality nuisance.

### Adds context [Research Square preprint — not peer-reviewed]: The Effects of High Carbonate Alkalinity under Low Salinity on the Survival, Physiological Metabolism, and Redox Homeostasis of Crassostrea hongkongensis
Nan et al., 2026 · DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10038928/v1 · https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10038928/v1

This preprint exposed Hong Kong oysters to elevated carbonate alkalinity under low salinity and found that higher alkalinity reduced survival, suppressed oxygen consumption and ammonia excretion, and altered redox and energy metabolism markers. Although the lab's trial tank showed the opposite extreme — alkalinity driven to zero rather than elevated — this preprint establishes that alkalinity level directly governs oysters' ability to excrete ammonia, suggesting the trial tank's combination of no measurable alkalinity and rising ammonia may not be coincidental but mechanistically linked.
