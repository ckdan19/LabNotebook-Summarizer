## Next steps for thesis project

**Source:** HazelAbrahamsonA, 2026-09-01 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/09/01/next-steps-for-thesis-project/
**Finding:** A 48-hour 32°C heat stress depletes glycogen in oysters, with recovery to control levels during subsequent non-stress recovery; however, using this same 32°C stress as a priming exposure does not reduce glycogen depletion upon a second exposure (no observed priming effect), and priming is similarly absent in 24- and 48-hour exposures at 35°C, with high mortality confounding the 48-hour 35°C trial.

### Supports [PubMed]: Effects of temperature and Nocardia crassostreae on the immune response of the Pacific oyster, Magallana gigas
Mason et al., 2026 · PMID: 42476329 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42476329/

Researchers exposed Pacific oysters (Magallana gigas) to elevated temperature and to the pathogen Nocardia crassostreae for 42 days, then measured energetic and immune parameters. Elevated temperature independently reduced glycogen and ATP concentrations, haemocyte viability, and gill Na+/K+-ATPase activity, showing that heat exposure alone depletes energy reserves regardless of infection status. This directly parallels the notebook's observation that heat stress depletes glycogen, and shows the effect generalizes across at least two independent experimental setups in this species.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Disrupting the cardiolipin oxidation-apoptosis axis: low-temperature, high-salinity depuration extends anhydrous storage in Pacific oysters
Meng et al., 2026 · PMID: 42637369 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42637369/

This study compared standard depuration against low-temperature, high-salinity depuration of Pacific oysters and found the cooler condition better preserved glycogen and ATP stores alongside higher survival, by limiting oxidative damage and apoptosis. It illustrates the inverse of the notebook's finding: cooler conditions conserve glycogen, consistent with heat itself — not general handling stress — being the driver of depletion. It does not test repeated or priming exposures.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Investigating the molecular mechanism of plasmalogen and quality changes in Pacific oyster (Crassostrea gigas) depurated under salinity stress
Song et al., 2026 · PMID: 42169268 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42169268/

This study exposed Pacific oysters to a range of salinities during depuration and found that high salinity increased glycogen content and upregulated glycolysis genes, while low salinity had the opposite effect. It shows glycogen reserves in this species respond broadly to abiotic stress beyond temperature alone, supporting the general premise that environmental stressors modulate energy metabolism — though it does not test heat stress or priming specifically.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Glycophagy is an ancient bilaterian pathway supporting metabolic adaptation through STBD1 structural evolution
Ren et al., 2026 · PMID: 41559265 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41559265/

This study identified the glycogen cargo receptor STBD1 in the Pacific oyster and showed it mediates glycophagy — autophagic mobilization of glycogen — during energy deprivation, tracing the mechanism's evolutionary origin across bilaterians. It offers a candidate molecular pathway underlying the glycogen depletion observed during heat stress, and suggests glycophagy/STBD1 activity as a target worth examining alongside the planned qPCR panel (HSP70/90, ATP synthase, citrate synthase).
