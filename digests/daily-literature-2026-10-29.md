## 10.28: GlycogenGlo Analysis (35 C repeat)

**Source:** HazelAbrahamsonA, 2026-10-28 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/10/28/10-28-glycogenglo-analysis-35-c-repeat/
**Finding:** In Crassostrea gigas, repeated 24-hour heat stress (35°C vs 15°C control) significantly decreased glycogen levels, with partial glycogen replenishment during a recovery period (not returning to control levels). Comparing oysters with and without a prior stress history exposed to a second heat stress event showed no significant difference in glycogen levels, suggesting that stress history does not significantly impact glycogen metabolism in subsequent stress events.

### Supports [PubMed]: Effects of temperature and Nocardia crassostreae on the immune response of the Pacific oyster, Magallana gigas
Mason G et al., 2026 · PMID: 42476329 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42476329/

Researchers exposed Pacific oysters (Magallana gigas, a synonym for Crassostrea gigas) to elevated temperature and/or the pathogen Nocardia crassostreae for 42 days and measured energetic and immune parameters. Elevated temperature alone significantly reduced mantle glycogen stores and ATP concentration independent of infection status, showing that thermal stress depletes energy reserves on its own. This directly corroborates the lab's observation that heat exposure drives down glycogen levels in the same species, here under a different (chronic, 42-day) thermal exposure.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Disrupting the cardiolipin oxidation-apoptosis axis: low-temperature, high-salinity depuration extends anhydrous storage in Pacific oysters
Meng N et al., 2026 · PMID: 42637369 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42637369/

This study compared standard versus low-temperature, high-salinity depuration conditions for anhydrous (air) storage of Pacific oysters, finding that the low-temperature, high-salinity regime better preserved glycogen and ATP levels alongside higher survival and upregulated Hsp70 expression. It shows glycogen depletion is a general physiological readout of stress in C. gigas across stress types (storage/anoxia, not just heat), and that protective conditions can spare glycogen reserves. This adds context to the lab's recovery-period observation by showing that glycogen sparing and depletion are a recognized axis of stress physiology in this species.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Investigating the molecular mechanism of plasmalogen and quality changes in Pacific oyster (Crassostrea gigas) depurated under salinity stress
Song Y et al., 2026 · PMID: 42169268 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42169268/

Oysters depurated across a salinity gradient (16-40 ppt) showed that high salinity increased glycogen content and immune capacity while low salinity reduced both, alongside shifts in plasmalogen lipid profiles and glycolysis gene expression. This demonstrates that glycogen content in C. gigas is bidirectionally and significantly modulated by abiotic stress severity, paralleling the lab's finding that stress exposure drives glycogen depletion. It adds context on how multiple stressor types beyond heat converge on glycogen as a shared physiological currency.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Glycophagy is an ancient bilaterian pathway supporting metabolic adaptation through STBD1 structural evolution
Ren L et al., 2026 · PMID: 41559265 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41559265/

This study identified and characterized the glycogen cargo receptor STBD1 in Crassostrea gigas and showed the species uses glycophagy (autophagic degradation of glycogen) to mobilize glycogen reserves during energy deprivation. It offers a candidate molecular mechanism for the glycogen depletion and partial recovery dynamics observed in the lab's heat-stress experiment, since glycophagy is one pathway by which stressed oysters could draw down glycogen stores for energy and potentially rebuild them during recovery. This is mechanistic context rather than a direct replication of the heat-stress/stress-history paradigm.
