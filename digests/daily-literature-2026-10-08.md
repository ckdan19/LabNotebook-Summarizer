## Tank room and survival assays

**Source:** Jesse Lowe, 2026-10-07 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/10/07/tank-room-and-survival-assays-13/
**Finding:** During a 72-hour mortality check of an ongoing oyster mortality assay, 12 oysters were still alive after full water changes were performed on tanks showing elevated nitrite (~3 ppm) and variable pH (7.4-8.0) and alkalinity (20-80) prior to the change.

### Adds context [SciELO Preprints preprint — not peer-reviewed]: Intravalvular oyster fluid as an innovation matrix for quantification of nitrite
Castro et al., 2026 · DOI: 10.1590/scielopreprints.15698 · https://doi.org/10.1590/scielopreprints.15698

This study measured nitrite (NO2-) concentrations directly in the intravalvular fluid of oysters across three estuarine sites with differing anthropogenic impact, alongside standard water-quality parameters like pH and dissolved oxygen. It found that oysters from the most impacted harvesting area accumulated significantly higher internal nitrite than those from less-impacted sites, validating oyster tissue nitrite as a biomonitoring signal for ambient nitrite exposure. This offers a comparative angle on the lab's tanks: it suggests that oysters can internally reflect elevated ambient nitrite (as seen in the ~3 ppm readings here) even when survival remains high, consistent with the 12 survivors recorded despite imperfect water chemistry.
