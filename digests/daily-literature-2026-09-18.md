Antoni et al., 2025 · PMID: 41852431 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41852431/

This mesocosm study compared two Ocean Alkalinity Enhancement (OAE) strategies — olivine weathering and dissolved sodium hydroxide (NaOH) addition, the same NaOH-based method used in the notebook's exposures — on European flat oysters (*Ostrea edulis*). It doesn't address growth or metabolic rate directly, but it indicates NaOH-based OAE is comparatively gentle ecologically, and flags gill microbiome shifts as another physiological axis worth watching alongside the growth/metabolism effects seen in Pacific and Olympia oysters.

## 09.17: ATP synthase/citrate synthase qPCR

**Source:** HazelAbrahamsonA, 2026-09-17 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/09/17/09-17-atp-synthase-citrate-synthase-qpcr/
**Finding:** In a 35°C repeated heat stress experiment, primed oysters (H/H) showed visually higher ATP synthase and citrate synthase expression than unprimed oysters (H/0, N/H), a pattern consistent with HSP70/HSP90 expression, but a prior experiment under different exposure conditions showed the opposite pattern for ATP synthase.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Protein Disulfide Isomerase A6 (PDIA6) Restrains Heat-Induced Oxidative Damage in Haemocytes of the Pacific Oyster (Crassostrea gigas)
Sun et al., 2026 · PMID: 42650228 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42650228/

Using transcriptomics, single-cell modeling, and RNAi knockdown, this study identified CgPDIA6 as a hub regulator linking heat-induced ER stress to oxidative damage and antioxidant enzyme activity (SOD1, SOD2, PRDX6) in Pacific oyster haemocytes exposed to 30°C. Silencing CgPDIA6 worsened oxidative injury and suppressed antioxidant gene expression, showing that redox/energy-stress regulators can shift markedly with heat exposure in this species. This supports the general principle behind the qPCR data — that heat stress reorganizes energy- and stress-related gene expression — though it examines a different regulatory node than ATP synthase or citrate synthase.

## 09.16: HSP70/90 qPCR and mortality evaluation

**Source:** HazelAbrahamsonA, 2026-09-16 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/09/16/09-16-hsp70-90-qpcr/
**Finding:** Repeated 35°C heat stress led to decreased HSP70 and HSP90 expression, with a smaller decrease in previously heat-primed (H/H) oysters than naive (N/H) oysters, suggesting a priming effect; however, a separate lethal heat-stress mortality experiment (37°C prime, 44°C challenge) showed 100% survival in both primed and unprimed groups, contrary to expectations from prior literature.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Tissue-Specific Trade-Offs Between Biomineralisation and Antioxidant Responses in Magallana gigas Infected with Boring Sponges Pione vastifica
Kladchenko et al., 2026 · PMID: 42193218 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42193218/

In Pacific oysters infested by a boring sponge, HSP70 and HSP90 expression decreased across all examined tissues, coinciding with a bioenergetic trade-off that reallocated limited ATP away from biomineralization toward antioxidant defense. This shows decreased HSP70/HSP90 expression in oysters isn't unique to heat exposure — it can also reflect broader resource-reallocation under stress — adding a useful alternative lens for interpreting the notebook's own decreased HSP70/HSP90 expression after repeated heat exposure.

### Adds context [bioRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed]: Parental immune priming reshapes offspring growth, metabolism, and thermal tolerance in the Pacific Oyster
Baird et al., 2025 · DOI: 10.64898/2025.12.10.693539 · https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.10.693539

This preprint found Pacific oyster offspring from immune-primed (Poly(I:C)-challenged) parents had 35% lower mortality than controls at 40°C but 17% higher mortality at 42°C, indicating priming benefits have thermal limits, and that primed offspring differentially expressed HSP70, HSP90, and viperin under acute heat stress. This closely parallels the notebook's own priming design and its finding of no survival difference between primed and unprimed oysters at a more extreme 44°C challenge — together suggesting heat-priming benefits (whether direct or, here, parental/transgenerational) may vanish or reverse once temperature exceeds a critical threshold, offering a plausible explanation for the notebook's "surprising" null mortality result.
