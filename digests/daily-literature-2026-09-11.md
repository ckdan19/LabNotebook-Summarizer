## Helping Megan with Resazurin Assays for Westcott and Agate Pass Manila Clams and Agate Pass Cockles

**Source:** gracehaaland, 2026-09-10 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/09/10/september-9th/
**Finding:** In resazurin-based survival assays on Manila clams collected from the Westcott site, nearly all individuals in the 40°C exposure group were dead by the 19-hour check, indicating a sharp acute thermal mortality threshold near 40°C.

### Supports [PubMed]: Integrating physiological and molecular approaches to assess thermal tolerance in the Manila clam (Ruditapes philippinarum)
Zhao et al., 2026 · PMID: 42035830 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42035830/

This study assessed thermal tolerance in Manila clams by monitoring heart rate, oxygen consumption, and antioxidant enzyme activity across heat-tolerant and non-tolerant strains held at 20, 26, and 32°C. Under 32°C stress over 504 hours, the heat-tolerant strain reached 49% survival while other (non-selected) strains fell below 10% survival, with the non-tolerant groups also showing severe cardiac impairment. Although the temperature and exposure duration differ from the lab's acute 40°C/19-hour trial, the pattern — non-selected Manila clams suffering overwhelming mortality under elevated thermal stress while a minority of resistant individuals survive — is consistent with the sharp mortality threshold observed in the Westcott assay.

### Adds context [Authorea Preprints preprint — not peer-reviewed]: A freezing tolerance landscape reveals opposing acute–chronic cold strategies in two intertidal clams
Zhang et al., 2026 · DOI: 10.22541/authorea.15003908/v1 · https://doi.org/10.22541/authorea.15003908/v1

This preprint applies a Thermal Tolerance Landscape (TTL) framework — integrating stress intensity and exposure duration — to quantify cumulative injury in Manila clams and varnish clams under sub-zero cold exposure, finding species-specific differences in how acute versus chronic cold stress accumulates into mortality. While the stressor (freezing, not heat) is the opposite end of the thermal spectrum from the lab's 40°C assay, the TTL approach — treating mortality as a joint function of temperature intensity and exposure time rather than a single LT50 — offers a relevant analytical framework for interpreting how the Westcott clams' mortality curve might shift if exposure duration or temperature were varied in future rounds.

**Literature summary:** One peer-reviewed paper (Zhao et al., 2026) supports the general pattern seen in the Westcott assay: Manila clams lacking heat-tolerance adaptations suffer severe mortality under thermal stress, though under different temperature/duration conditions (chronic 32°C vs. acute 40°C). One preprint (Zhang et al., 2026, not yet peer-reviewed) adds a methodological framework — thermal tolerance landscapes — from a cold-stress study in the same species that could inform how future dose/duration variations in the resazurin assay are interpreted.
