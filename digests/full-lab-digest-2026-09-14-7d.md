### The Pathogen Has a Plan, the Hosts Do Not
- **Author**: Steven Roberts
- **Date**: 2026-09-11
- **Categories**: Genomics
- **Blob**: https://github.com/sr320/tumbling-oysters/blob/d7bf0af7b72a2bed4fa13a1bf3df80b8f05fdd66/posts/87-seastar-susceptibility-genomics/index.qmd
- **Key finding**: Comparing the newly confirmed sea star wasting disease agent (*Vibrio pectenicida* FHCF-3) against three sea star genomes spanning a susceptibility gradient (sunflower > ochre > leather star), the pathogen's virulence arsenal is rich and complete — 16 pore-forming toxin/hemolysin loci, two intact aerolysin-family toxins, and fully functional T2SS/T3SS/T6SS delivery systems. On the host side, none of 50 tested gene families survive multiple-testing correction, so the susceptibility gradient is not explained by gene content, and two receptor-pathway hypotheses (GPI-anchor and sphingolipid biosynthesis) are positively excluded since both are intact and undifferentiated across all three species. The strongest surviving (non-significant) candidates are a host aerolysin-like β-PFT gene family and C-type lectins, both tracking the resistance gradient, motivating planned RNA-seq and coelomocyte toxin-sensitivity follow-up work.

### Manchester Hardening project lab survival testing results
- **Date**: 2026-09-08
- **Author**: Ariana Huffmyer
- **Categories**: manchester-hardening, experimental design
- **AI-use disclosure**: L0 (None)
- **Key finding**: Binomial logistic regression on five rounds of heat-survival trials (accounting for round and oyster length as random effects) found a significant family x hardening-treatment x time interaction, indicating that prior heat-hardening history affects survival in a family-specific way. Four of ten families showed significant treatment effects — two (families 137, 153) had improved tolerance (longer LT50) after hardening while two (families 116, 163) had reduced tolerance — with the rest showing no hardening effect, supporting the conclusion that hardening benefits are highly family-dependent rather than universal.

### VIMS Diploid/Triploid performance-resazurin correlations
- **Date**: 2026-09-10
- **Author**: Ariana Huffmyer
- **Categories**: resazurin, vims, metabolism, cvirginica
- **AI-use disclosure**: L2 (Drafting/Coding)
- **Key finding**: Spearman correlations between resazurin metabolic curve features and field performance metrics (condition index, weight, growth) in ~160 diploid and triploid *C. virginica* oysters outplanted for a year at VIMS revealed opposite relationships between ploidy groups. Diploids show negative correlations between metabolic activity (peak metabolism, metabolic acceleration, inflection time) and condition index, suggesting a metabolism-performance trade-off, whereas triploids show positive correlations, suggesting they escape this trade-off — possibly because they lack the energetic cost of reproduction.

### Vibrio pectenicida virulence genomics and sea star wasting disease susceptibility

**Source:** Tumbling Oysters (Steven Roberts)
**Finding:** Comparing *Vibrio pectenicida* FHCF-3's virulence gene content against three sea star genomes spanning a susceptibility gradient (sunflower > ochre > leather star), the pathogen has a complete virulence arsenal, but none of 50 host gene families tested survive multiple-testing correction — the susceptibility gradient is not explained by host gene content, and the GPI-anchor and sphingolipid receptor-pathway hypotheses are excluded.

### [Adds context] [PubMed]: Microbe Profile: Vibrio pectenicida: the deadly marine bacteria with strains impacting sea stars and scallops
Blackwood et al., 2026 · PMID: 42455634 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42455634/

This microbe-profile review characterizes *V. pectenicida* as a marine bacterium with multiple strain types, confirming strain FHCF-3 as the agent associated with sea star wasting disease and noting a separate strain (A365) causes larval scallop mortality via a distinct toxin. It provides taxonomic and strain-level background for the lab's genomic comparison but does not itself analyze virulence gene content or host susceptibility.

### Family-dependent survival benefit from heat hardening in Pacific oysters

**Source:** Ariana Huffmyer Lab Notebook (cross-referenced with genefish WordPress's Manchester Round 5 survival data)
**Finding:** A binomial logistic regression across five rounds of heat-survival trials found a significant family x hardening-treatment x time interaction in *Crassostrea gigas*: only 4 of 10 families showed a significant hardening effect, with two families gaining tolerance and two losing it, indicating hardening benefits are highly family-dependent rather than universal.

### [Adds context] [PubMed]: Epitranscriptomic erasers in bivalves: Evolutionary divergence and species-specific transcriptional plasticity of the ALKBH family under acute thermal stress
Xiong et al., 2026 · PMID: 42322884 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42322884/

This study profiles ALKBH RNA-demethylase genes across 35 bivalve species and finds that *Crassostrea gigas* maintains sustained ALKBH3 expression under heat shock, a pattern the authors link to its comparatively strong thermal tolerance relative to a subtropical scallop. It doesn't examine family-line variation within *C. gigas*, but supports the idea that thermal-tolerance differences in this species have a molecular/epigenetic basis, consistent with the lab's finding that hardening responses vary by genetic background.

**Literature summary:** One peer-reviewed paper provides molecular context for species-level thermal tolerance in *Crassostrea gigas* but does not address within-species, family-line variation in hardening response — the lab's family-dependent finding does not yet have a direct literature parallel at that resolution.

### Late-onset mortality following OAE exposure in Pacific oysters

**Source:** genefish WordPress
**Finding:** Adult Pacific oysters exposed to ocean alkalinity enhancement (OAE) for 24 hours and returned to ambient conditions showed only 1/39 mortality at a 2-day checkpoint but 16/39 dead by one week — a delayed-onset mortality pattern consistent with prior adult and juvenile OAE trials in this lab.

### [Conflicts] [PubMed]: Differential impacts of ocean acidification and alkalinization on shell microstructure and molecular responses in Mytilus edulis
Chen et al., 2026 · PMID: 41806513 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41806513/

This study exposed blue mussels (*Mytilus edulis*) to short-term (21-day) ocean acidification or NaOH-based ocean alkalinization and found survival rates were unaffected by either treatment, with alkalinization actually enhancing shell integrity and growth-related gene expression. This conflicts on its face with the lab's observed late-onset OAE mortality in oysters, but the species (mussel vs. oyster), OAE chemistry (NaOH-based vs. the lab's own method), and exposure/monitoring duration all differ, so the discrepancy may reflect species-specific sensitivity or a lack of the longer-term monitoring window needed to catch delayed effects.

**Literature summary:** One peer-reviewed paper reports no mortality effect from short-term ocean alkalinization in a related bivalve, apparently conflicting with the lab's delayed-mortality finding in Pacific oysters — but given the cross-species and cross-protocol differences, this warrants direct comparison rather than treatment as a true contradiction.
