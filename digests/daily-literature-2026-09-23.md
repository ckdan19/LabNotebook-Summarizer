## Sept 21st: Oyster Inventory, Combining El Niño Bags, and Resazurin Assays

**Source:** robertsblr, 2026-09-21 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/09/21/sept-21st-oyster-inventory-combining-el-nino-bags-and-resazurin-assays/
**Finding:** Oysters were run through resazurin viability assays under salt-water, agitation, and 42°C heat-stress conditions with readings every 15 minutes over a 2-hour time course, while parallel mortality checks showed differential survival between El Niño-exposed oyster bags (32 alive / 9 dead) and undisturbed stock bins (e.g. 118 alive / 18 dead in one bin, 48 alive with no reported deaths in a control bin).

### Adds context [PubMed]: Cytotoxicity of polystyrene nanoplastics involves mitochondrial dysfunction and DNA damage in hemocytes of the Pacific oyster
de Carvalho Penha et al., 2025 · PMID: 41115343 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41115343/

This study used a resazurin metabolic-activity assay alongside a neutral red lysosomal-integrity assay to characterize cytotoxicity of polystyrene nanoplastics in hemocytes of the Pacific oyster (*Crassostrea gigas*), finding the resazurin assay more sensitive (LC50 = 91.6 mg/L) than the lysosomal assay. It further showed that shifting cells toward aerobic metabolism increased nanoplastic-induced cytotoxicity and DNA damage, while promoting anaerobic metabolism reduced these effects. While the stressor differs (nanoplastics rather than heat), the paper reinforces that resazurin-based metabolic readouts are a validated, sensitive way to detect stress-induced physiological disruption in this species, supporting the notebook's use of the assay as a stress indicator.

### Supports [bioRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed]: From Blue to Pink: Resazurin as a High-Throughput Proxy for Metabolic Rate in Oysters
Huffmyer et al., 2025 · DOI: 10.1101/2025.11.06.686367 · https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.06.686367

This preprint directly validates the resazurin assay as a metabolic-rate proxy in *Crassostrea gigas* and *C. virginica*, confirming that resazurin fluorescence correlates with oxygen consumption and characterizing acute thermal stress responses. It reports that oysters showing greater metabolic depression under acute heat stress were more likely to survive, and that family-level genetic variation in metabolic stress response is detectable. This closely mirrors the notebook's pairing of 42°C resazurin readings with parallel survival/mortality checks, and suggests the collected time-course data could be analyzed against the El Niño-bag mortality outcomes to test whether metabolic depression predicts survival in this population as well.

**Literature summary:** One peer-reviewed paper and one preprint both support treating resazurin fluorescence as a valid, sensitive readout of metabolic/stress state in oysters, though only the preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) directly addresses acute thermal stress and links metabolic depression to survival — the same pairing of assay and mortality data seen in this notebook entry. The peer-reviewed paper adds broader context that resazurin-based assays reliably detect stress-induced metabolic disruption in Pacific oyster tissue, even for a different stressor (nanoplastics).
