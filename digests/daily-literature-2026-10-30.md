### Adds context [PubMed]: The ammonia transporter gene family and its immune regulatory role in the Pacific oyster (Crassostrea gigas)
Sun P et al., 2026 · PMID: 41861978 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41861978/

This study characterized the ammonia transporter (AMT) gene family in *Crassostrea gigas* and showed that sustained ammonia exposure (via long-term *Vibrio splendidus* stimulation) drives progressive ammonia accumulation alongside haemocyte death and coordinated upregulation of DNA-repair, ion-transport, and protein-synthesis genes. It provides a mechanistic basis for treating the trial tank's persistently elevated ammonia (0.25+ ppm) as a meaningful physiological stressor to the oysters housed there rather than a benign reading, since sustained ammonia exposure is linked here to immune-cell death in this same species.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Ecological functions of sea cucumber Apostichopus japonicus in oyster-kelp-sea cucumber IMTA mode: Organic matter extracting and disturbance-driven nutrient release
Xie Y et al., 2026 · PMID: 41921340 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41921340/

This study measured ammonia excretion and dissolved inorganic nitrogen (DIN) fluxes around *Crassostrea gigas* in an integrated multi-trophic aquaculture system, finding that excretion rates and nutrient-flux dynamics vary with body size and temperature. It is only loosely connected to the lab's finding — it addresses nitrogen cycling in an oyster-adjacent culture system rather than water-chemistry degradation inside a holding tank — but it does show that ammonia/DIN levels around *C. gigas* are dynamic and sensitive to husbandry conditions, which is the same general phenomenon (fluctuating tank nitrogen chemistry) the lab is tracking.

### Adds context [Research Square preprint — not peer-reviewed]: The Effects of High Carbonate Alkalinity under Low Salinity on the Survival, Physiological Metabolism, and Redox Homeostasis of Crassostrea hongkongensis
Nan X et al., 2026 · DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-10038928/v1 · https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10038928/v1

Oysters (*Crassostrea hongkongensis*, a close relative of *C. gigas*) exposed to elevated carbonate alkalinity (10 or 20 mmol/L) under low salinity showed suppressed oxygen consumption and ammonia excretion, reduced survival at the higher alkalinity level, and redox/energy-metabolism remodeling (altered catalase, glutathione peroxidase, glucose, ATP). This is directly relevant context for the trial tank: it demonstrates that elevated alkalinity and disrupted ammonia handling are not independent nuisance readings but a combined physiological stressor that can measurably harm oyster survival and metabolism in a related *Crassostrea* species.
