### Adds context [PubMed]: Olivine and dissolved alkalinity trigger different bacterial community shifts in water and oyster gills: insights from a mesocosm experiment
Antoni et al., 2025 · PMID: 41852431 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41852431/

This mesocosm study chronically exposed European flat oysters (*Ostrea edulis*) to alkalinity-enhanced seawater generated either by olivine weathering or dissolved NaOH addition, and tracked bacterial community shifts in the water and oyster gill microbiome. It found that alkalization method and concentration significantly reshaped both the surrounding water microbiome and the oyster gill microbiome, with olivine-based OAE producing larger, and in some respects riskier, shifts than dissolved-alkalinity OAE. This supports the general premise behind the lab's observation — that prior OAE exposure leaves a physiological or microbial "signature" on oysters that can shape how they respond to a later stressor — though this paper measures microbiome composition rather than metabolic rate.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Oyster farming acts as a marine carbon dioxide removal (mCDR) hotspot for climate change mitigation
Chen et al., 2025 · PMID: 40892922 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40892922/

Using field mesocosms, this study shows that oyster filter-feeding activity itself drives local seawater toward a more alkaline, autotrophic state by accelerating organic carbon production and deposition — effectively making oyster culture a carbon-removal process rather than only a passive recipient of alkalinity changes. It is directly relevant background for interpreting an OAE exposure experiment, since it establishes that oysters and seawater alkalinity/carbonate chemistry interact bidirectionally, but it does not address metabolic rate under heat stress and so neither confirms nor contradicts the lab's specific resazurin result.
