## Literature Connections

> Note: this section performs live PubMed and bioRxiv searches for each notable finding. Only findings with at least one relevant retrieved paper are shown.

### Manchester hardening: family-level acute thermal survival at 36 °C

**Source:** Ariana Huffmyer Lab Notebook; Genefish WordPress (Jesse Lowe, naomikang44, maddyab)
**Finding:** Ten Pacific oyster families heat-stressed sublethally in June 2025 and outplanted for a year at Manchester were subsampled into 20 blinded family/treatment bags and held at 36 °C to near-total mortality; round 1 ran to ~97% mortality in three days, with survival scored twice daily per cup.

### Supports [PubMed]: Embryonic Hormetic Priming Modulates Later-Life Thermal Tolerance
Lugue et al., 2026 · PMID: 42017008 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42017008/

Two bi-parental families of black-lip pearl oyster were heat-primed as embryos, raised four months under common conditions, then challenged at a sublethal 34 °C. Priming raised spat thermal tolerance in one family and reduced it in the other, while the core heat-response pathways stayed conserved across families — the priming signature showed up instead as reorganization of unfolded-protein-response gene networks. That family-dependent sign flip is the central caution for reading the Manchester rounds: a hardened-versus-control contrast may only be interpretable family by family, which is exactly why the 20 bags are kept as separate blinded units.

### Supports [PubMed]: Transgenerational plasticity responses of larvae of Sydney rock oysters (Saccostrea glomerata) to ocean warming
Filippini et al., 2026 · PMID: 41202725 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41202725/

Adult Sydney rock oysters from two genetically distinct families were conditioned at 24 °C or 28 °C for about eight weeks, and their larvae were then assayed across 24.3–36.0 °C. Parental warming raised larval LT50 by roughly 2 °C in one of the two families but not the other, alongside better development and lower abnormality at cost of smaller size. This is a second independent report that a thermal-priming benefit is family-contingent, and it demonstrates the LT50-style summary statistic the Manchester time-to-death data could be reduced to.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Short-term heat stress adaptation in intertidal oysters (Crassostrea sikamea): Integrative biochemical, transcriptomic and metabolomic insights
Liu et al., 2026 · PMID: 41401620 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41401620/

Kumamoto oyster gill tissue was profiled biochemically, transcriptomically, and metabolomically from 0.5 h through the onset of shell gaping under a ladder of sublethal-to-lethal temperatures, paired with a survival assay. Mortality was minimal below 45 °C but survival collapsed to 14% after one hour at 45 °C, and antioxidant activity was only significantly induced from 39 °C upward. This maps the temperature–time tradeoff around the Manchester protocol's choice of a sustained 36 °C hold: at that temperature mortality accrues over days rather than hours, so the assay is measuring cumulative tolerance rather than an acute lethal threshold.

### Supports [bioRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed]: Parental immune priming reshapes offspring growth, metabolism, and thermal tolerance in the Pacific Oyster
Baird et al., 2025 · DOI: 10.64898/2025.12.10.693539 · https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.10.693539

Pacific oyster broodstock were given a Poly(I:C) immune challenge and their offspring reared to seed stage, then assessed for survival, growth, and metabolic response under thermal stress. Offspring of challenged parents had 35% lower mortality than controls at 40 °C but 17% higher mortality at 42 °C, and 43% higher metabolic activity at 36 °C versus 30% lower at 40 °C. The reversal with assay temperature is directly cautionary for a single-temperature design: at 36 °C specifically, primed animals were the metabolically elevated group, so a 36 °C endpoint may not rank treatments the same way a hotter one would.

### Suggests next step [EcoEvoRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed]: Intertidal Exposure Modulates Time-Integrated Heat Tolerance of the Eastern Oyster Crassostrea virginica
Villeneuve et al., 2026 · DOI: 10.32942/x2cw9d · https://doi.org/10.32942/x2cw9d

Thermal death time curves were built for eastern oysters under immersion and emersion using heart-rate monitoring to determine time to death, then combined into exposure-specific tolerance landscapes and a tidal-cycle survival model that reproduced observed field survival after calibrating for field acclimation. Submerged animals outlasted emersed ones at lower lethal temperatures but the ranking inverted above 37 °C. The framework suggests fitting the Manchester twice-daily scoring as time-integrated tolerance curves per family rather than comparing endpoint mortality, which would also let the year of field acclimation at Manchester be modeled explicitly instead of treated as noise.

**Literature summary:** Three peer-reviewed papers and two preprints converge on a single message for this assay: thermal-priming effects in oysters are family-specific in sign, not just in magnitude (peer-reviewed evidence in *Pinctada margaritifera* and *Saccostrea glomerata*), which supports keeping the 20 Manchester bags as independent units and argues against pooling hardened versus control across families. Two preprints add that the answer is temperature-dependent — a Pacific oyster priming benefit at 40 °C reversed at 42 °C, and eastern oyster tolerance rankings inverted above 37 °C — so a single 36 °C endpoint should be reported as one point on a tolerance landscape rather than as a general ranking. The *C. sikamea* work places 36 °C in the cumulative-stress rather than acute-lethal regime, consistent with round 1 taking three days to reach near-total mortality.

### Population-level DNA methylation differences in *Ostrea chilensis*

**Source:** Tumbling Oysters (Steven Roberts)
**Finding:** Whole-genome bisulfite data from two Chilean sites (Quihua, Rio Pudeto; n = 4 each) yielded 62 differentially methylated CpGs out of 492,229 tested, with 61 of 62 showing complete group non-overlap, no detectable difference in global methylation, and a chromosome 1B hotspot — but site is confounded with every environmental and population-genetic variable.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Prior exposure to hypoxia alters DNA methylation patterns in the eastern oyster
McDonough et al., 2026 · PMID: 41942905 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41942905/ (originally posted as bioRxiv preprint: https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.15.694390)

Juvenile eastern oysters were exposed to control or hypoxic conditions at two timepoints 60 days apart in a fully factorial design and profiled by methylRAD. Methylation sat mostly in exons, both the first and the second exposure contributed significantly to gene-body methylation variance, and animals moved from hypoxia to control diverged most from every other group; differentially methylated genes fell in oxidative stress, metabolism, and transcription. The result that environmental history registers as locus-specific gene-body change is consistent with the Chilean pattern of indistinguishable global methylation alongside 62 clean site-discriminating loci — and it sharpens the stated caveat, since it shows past environment alone can generate exactly this signal without any heritable difference.

### Suggests next step [PubMed]: Bonamia ostreae in New Zealand: Lessons From a Decade-Long Marine Biosecurity Response
Pande et al., 2026 · PMID: 42522055 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42522055/

This account of New Zealand's ten-year response to *Bonamia ostreae* in *Ostrea chilensis* describes near-total depopulation of farmed stock to protect the Foveaux Strait wild fishery, identifies fragmented data systems as a key constraint, and names resilience breeding among the mitigation strategies worth pursuing. Resilience breeding in this species presupposes exactly the kind of population- and site-level baseline the Quihua/Rio Pudeto comparison is generating, and it puts a practical premium on the open question the post flags — whether site differences are plastic or heritable, since only the heritable component is selectable.

**Literature summary:** Recent indexed literature on *Ostrea chilensis* is dominated by *Bonamia* biosecurity rather than genomics, so there is no direct comparison for a site-contrast DML analysis in this species. The closest peer-reviewed anchor is the eastern oyster hypoxia work, which independently supports the locus-specific-not-global pattern seen here while also demonstrating that prior environment alone suffices to produce it — reinforcing rather than resolving the post's own confounding caveat. No preprints on oyster DNA methylation beyond the published version of that same study were retrieved.
