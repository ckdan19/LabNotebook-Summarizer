## Tumbling Oysters (Steven Roberts)

### Lean vs Lean
- **Date**: 10-16-2026
- **Author**: Steven Roberts
- **Categories**: Genomics, Computing
- **Key finding**: Steven compared the lab's lean lake trout genome directly against the published lean reference (SaNama_1.0, a single homozygous lean haplotype) to establish a baseline for how much lean-vs-lean variation should be expected, as a control for the ongoing lean-vs-siscowet ecotype comparison. At the sequence level (SNPs, diverged blocks), lean-vs-reference differences were about as large as lean-vs-siscowet differences, and of ~100,000 lean-siscowet structural variants, roughly a quarter matched the "lean side" (i.e., driven by lean being the odd one out, not a true ecotype signal). Critically, nearly all 115 reciprocal presence/absence "genes" turned out to be intronic structural variation rather than true gene loss, narrowing the energy-metabolism candidate list to two genes (`gpia` and `grb10`-like) with real coding-sequence changes.

### Collagen/extracellular-matrix gene expression and species-specific resilience to sea star wasting disease (SSWD)

**Source:** Genefish WordPress (Samuel Slutz, "Starfish Interspecies Comparative Transcriptomics Project; Where It's Been and Where It's Headed")
**Finding:** Comparative transcriptomics across three sea star species (Pisaster ochraceus, Pycnopodia helianthoides, Dermasterias imbricata) tests the hypothesis that the more SSWD-resilient D. imbricata shows less downregulation of collagen/extracellular-matrix genes upon disease exposure than the more vulnerable species.

### Adds context [PubMed]: A Comparison of Juvenile and Adult Sunflower Sea Star Transcriptomic Responses to Challenge With Sea Star Wasting Disease Tissue Homogenates
Crandall et al., 2026 · PMID: 42780574 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42780574/

This study (co-authored by Grace Crandall and Steven Roberts, both represented elsewhere in this digest) experimentally challenged juvenile and adult Pycnopodia helianthoides with SSWD-affected tissue homogenates and compared transcriptomic responses by age class. Both age classes developed consistent disease signs, and while some age-driven differentially expressed genes emerged, a shared "core" transcriptional response to SSWD challenge was identified across trials, including genes tied to defense against bacteria. For the lab's cross-species collagen-expression comparison, this suggests age class is a variable worth controlling for when comparing transcriptomic responses to SSWD challenge across species.

### Adds context [PubMed]: Precursors of sea star wasting: immune and microbial disruption during initial disease outbreak in southeast Alaska
McCracken et al., 2026 · PMID: 42014077 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42014077/

Using wild Pycnopodia helianthoides sampled during an active SSWD outbreak, this study found that individuals exposed to the disease but not yet showing visible signs already had altered expression of extracellular matrix and tissue-remodeling genes, alongside immune activation, correlating with shifts in Vibrio and other microbial abundances. This supports the idea that ECM/collagen-related gene expression is an early, biologically meaningful signal during SSWD progression rather than a downstream consequence of visible tissue damage, consistent with the lab's choice to focus on collagen genes as a resilience marker.

**Literature summary:** Two peer-reviewed papers, both using the same focal species (Pycnopodia helianthoides) as the lab's ongoing three-species comparison, support treating collagen/ECM gene expression as a biologically meaningful early signal in SSWD progression, while also flagging age class as a potential confound in cross-species transcriptomic comparisons. No preprints were found on this topic in the last 12 months. Notably, one of the two papers is co-authored by Grace Crandall and Steven Roberts, tying this literature search directly back to the lab's own prior published work.
