## Checking Oyster Mortality in El Niño Experiment

**Source:** robertsblr, 2026-09-18 · https://genefish.wordpress.com/2026/09/18/checking-oyster-mortality-in-el-nino-experiment/
**Finding:** In a simulated El Niño (elevated temperature) exposure experiment, Pacific oyster bags showed substantially higher and rapidly worsening mortality (several bags exceeding 50% dead within roughly 3 hours between the 10am and 1pm counts) compared to control bags, which maintained low mortality (about 2-12%) over the same period.

Adds context [PubMed]: Microbial endolithic symbiosis in oysters enhances thermal resistance through shell corrosion
Zardi et al., 2026 · PMID: 42430847 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42430847/

This study found that Pacific oysters (Magallana gigas) colonized by shell-corroding endolithic symbionts had significantly higher survival under heat stress in both lab and field experiments, with corrosion-related shell lightening providing a thermal buffer as high as 9.5°C. It doesn't test rapid multi-hour mortality onset directly, but it shows survival under acute thermal/climate stress in this species can vary dramatically depending on a structural, symbiont-mediated trait — a plausible source of the sharp bag-to-bag variation in mortality rate seen in the El Niño exposure.

Adds context [bioRxiv preprint — not peer-reviewed]: Parental immune priming reshapes offspring growth, metabolism, and thermal tolerance in the Pacific Oyster
Baird et al., 2025 · DOI: 10.64898/2025.12.10.693539 · https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.10.693539

This preprint found that Pacific oyster offspring under elevated temperature had 35% lower mortality than controls at 40°C but 17% higher mortality at 42°C, indicating mortality risk in this species rises sharply and non-linearly once a thermal threshold is crossed. This is consistent with the notebook's observation of El Niño-exposed bags going from moderate to severe mortality within just a few hours, suggesting the animals may have crossed a similar critical thermal threshold during the exposure window.

**Literature summary:** One peer-reviewed paper and one not-yet-peer-reviewed bioRxiv preprint from the last 12 months bear on this finding, though neither directly tested a simulated El Niño-style warming exposure or hour-scale mortality onset. Together they suggest Pacific oyster mortality under thermal/climate stress is highly threshold-dependent and can vary substantially with individual traits such as symbiont-mediated shell corrosion — both plausible contributors to the sharp divergence between control and El Niño-exposed bags observed here.
